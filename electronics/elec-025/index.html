Key Facts

Forward voltage basics

LEDs require a minimum forward voltage (Vf) to conduct and emit light. Unlike resistors, they do not self-limit current — always use a series resistor or constant-current driver. Vf varies with color (semiconductor material) and increases slightly at lower temperatures.

Standard vs. high-brightness

Standard LEDs (5 mm, ~20 mA) have Vf values listed here. High-power LEDs (1 W, 3 W, 5 W) typically have similar Vf but run at 350 mA–1 A — use a constant-current driver. SMD LEDs (0402–5050) have similar Vf, check the datasheet for If_max.

Resistor formula

R = (Vcc − Vf) / If

Example: Vcc = 5 V, red LED Vf = 2.0 V, If = 20 mA:
R = (5 − 2.0) / 0.020 = 150 Ω → use 150 Ω (E24)

White LED note

White LEDs use a blue or UV GaN die with a phosphor coating. Their Vf is therefore similar to blue LEDs (~2.9–3.5 V), not 2 V. Using a 2 V resistor calculation for white LEDs will over-drive them.

RGB LED — wiring and Vf per channel

Common cathode vs. common anode

  • Common cathode: all cathodes share GND; each anode gets its own resistor to Vcc — easier with GPIO HIGH = ON
  • Common anode: all anodes share Vcc; each cathode gets its own resistor to GND — GPIO LOW = ON (inverted logic)
  • 5 mm RGB through-hole: 4 pins (R, G, B, common)

Calculate resistors per channel

  • Red: Vf ≈ 2.0 V → R = (5−2.0)/0.020 = 150 Ω
  • Green: Vf ≈ 3.2 V → R = (5−3.2)/0.020 = 91 Ω
  • Blue: Vf ≈ 3.2 V → R = (5−3.2)/0.020 = 91 Ω
  • Each channel needs its own resistor — never share one
IR & UV LEDs — safety and usage

Infrared LEDs

  • 850 nm / 940 nm most common (remote controls, sensors)
  • Invisible to naked eye — use a camera/phone to verify emission
  • Vf lower than visible LEDs (~1.2–1.5 V); calculate resistor carefully
  • Often pulsed at high currents (up to 1 A peak) in burst mode

UV LEDs

  • 365–405 nm: resin curing, banknote verification, fluorescence
  • Do not look directly into UV LED — eye damage risk
  • High Vf (3.0–4.5 V) — similar to blue/violet GaN
  • 395–405 nm "near UV" is safer for general use than 365 nm
